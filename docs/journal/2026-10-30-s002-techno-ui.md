# Session 002: Techno UI

The first study session. The Steward had created the folder `session-techno-ui/` before the project's rig existed; this session fills it.

## Intent

The Steward's brief, in Russian: in this folder we experiment with a visual, shader-backed musical image for an interface. The base was pasted from the Steward's other conversation about a portal specification: its section 9, "Page score", maps a track onto a page. Beat 8 px, bar 32 px, phrase 64 px; layers lead, bass, hat, pad; sidechain, masking, breakdown, progressive layering and swing as rules; budgets and gates; five templates with track forms. The ask: take the base and develop it into a techno image with visual shader backing.

## What happened

- The Model wrote [session-techno-ui/techno-score.md](../../session-techno-ui/techno-score.md): why techno in particular (fixed grid, few layers, change by subtraction and addition, loudness by ducking, repetition with permitted variation), the grid tokens, the layers with their treatments and gates, the dynamics, the track forms of the five templates, the arrangement of the demo, the uniform mapping, open points.
- The Model built [session-techno-ui/shader/](../../session-techno-ui/shader/): a synthesised techno loop on the Web Audio API (kick with a falling pitch and a click, closed and open hats from filtered noise, a rolling bass with a sub octave, a detuned pad chord, a lead stab, a riser) on a lookahead scheduler; a page layout of twenty rectangles on the 8 px grid with layers and bars of entry; four live gates; a fragment shader on WebGL 1 that draws the page by layer from the audio envelopes.
- `Makefile` gained `techno` and `serve-techno`; `.claude/launch.json` gained the `techno-ui` preview; `GLOSSARY.md` gained the session's terms.

## What we heard

The Model cannot listen. It verified that the engine ran: the AudioContext at 48 000 Hz in state `running`, an output latency of 24 ms, two to three hits queued ahead of the clock at every check, the pad level and the duck gain following the sections. Whether the kick sits right, whether the bass rolls and whether the clash beats as intended is for the Steward's ear.

## What we saw

Tested in the Claude Code browser pane at 800 px wide (the stage is 960 px, so the right edge scrolled):

| Bar | Section | Seen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | intro | cold blue accent; only the state line with its underline and the two aside surfaces; the grid faint |
| 7 | build | the caption appeared under the state line; the grid pulsing; accent shifted cooler |
| 11 | drop | accent `hsl(26 78% 56%)`; the Approve button landed in orange; rows one and two arrived with their captions; the sixteenth scan visible as a brighter segment running along the row |
| 18 | breakdown | violet accent; the rows collapsed to a trace; the lead glowed and cast light on its surroundings; the aside swelled |
| 20 | breakdown, clash on | a second button, "Reject all", beside the first; the **one lead** and **masking** gates failed; interference visible between the two blocks; off again, both gates passed |

No console messages at any point. The shader compiled first time.

## Decisions

- Level L2: rules of musical form used to build, then checked by eye and ear. Position Director: the Steward sets the image and judges it.
- The kick is not a page element; it is the grid, and the grid pulses with it.
- The spacing token `--phrase` stays at 64 px (two bars); the four-bar budget unit is called a period. Naming left open for the Steward.
- The clash is a deliberate violation kept in the demo as a negative example, because the gates are best understood by failing them.
- The accent hue shifts per section in the demo; a product would keep one.
- The texts on the page (orders, payouts, "Decided by Finance") are illustrative and were made up for the demo; they are not from the Steward's product.
